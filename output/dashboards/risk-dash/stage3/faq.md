## 8.1 Frequently Asked Questions

---

**Why is the Eligible Population number different from the Members count?**
Eligible Population counts all attributed members who qualify for risk measurement, while Members reflects those with at least one documented risk score on file. A widening gap between the two means a growing share of your attributed population has no documented risk at all — treat this as an early warning that outreach coverage is falling behind.

---

**Why is Documented Risk lower than Potential Risk, and should I be concerned?**
Documented Risk is the portion of clinical complexity your organization has actually captured in claims and encounters; Potential Risk sets the ceiling based on all identified conditions, including suspected and historical ones. The distance between them is exactly what Gap to Potential Risk measures — if that gap is large or growing, your documentation workflows are not keeping pace with the clinical complexity of the population.

---

**What is Gap to Potential Risk, and why does it matter more than the other risk KPIs?**
Gap to Potential Risk is the single most actionable number on the Overview page — it directly quantifies how much documentation work remains before the coding window closes. Always read it alongside Dropped + Suspected to understand whether the gap is being driven by conditions that fell off from prior years (a recapture problem) or by newly identified suspected conditions (an outreach problem), because each requires a different intervention.

---

**What is the difference between Dropped and Suspected conditions, and why does it change what I do next?**
Dropped conditions are diagnoses that were coded in a prior period but have not yet been recaptured this year, signaling a recapture failure that annual visit coding can address. Suspected conditions are net-new clinical signals — from claims history, lab results, or risk models — that have never been formally documented, requiring targeted member outreach and provider engagement. If your Gap to Potential Risk is dominated by Suspected conditions, prioritize outreach campaigns; if it is dominated by Dropped conditions, focus on closing annual visit coding gaps with existing providers.

---

**My Risk Recapture Rate looks strong, but % Members with Open Coding Gaps is still high. Is something wrong?**
Not necessarily — but this pattern is a warning sign. A high recapture rate paired with a high open gap rate means your team is closing known gaps but new or persistent gaps are accumulating faster than they are being resolved. Monitor % Members with Open Coding Gaps as a leading indicator: if it stays elevated as the measurement year progresses, both Risk and RAF Recapture Rates are at risk of falling short of targets by year end.

---

**Risk Recapture Rate and RAF Recapture Rate are moving in opposite directions. What does that mean?**
When these two rates diverge — one rising while the other falls — it signals a mismatch between the volume and the acuity of recaptured conditions. For example, your team may be closing a high number of low-acuity gaps while missing fewer but higher-weight conditions. Navigate to the Disease Category breakdown in the Bottom layer to identify which condition categories are driving the acuity shortfall.

---

**PMPM is rising, but recapture rates are flat. Should I escalate this?**
Yes. A rising PMPM paired with a rising RAF Recapture Rate is expected — cost is tracking with appropriately documented acuity. But a rising PMPM with flat or declining recapture rates may indicate unmanaged utilization that is not being offset by risk documentation. Bring this pattern to your Medical Director and cross-reference it with the Payer/Plan Segmentation on the Top layer to identify whether the cost pressure is concentrated in a specific plan or population segment.

---

**A payer shows a large Gap to Potential Risk and a low recapture rate. Where do I go next?**
Navigate to the Action layer's Payer/Plan Opportunity Summary. If that payer also surfaces there with high targetable revenue opportunity, the underperformance is not just a documentation issue — it is a recoverable revenue gap. Prioritize member outreach and provider engagement within that payer's attributed population before the coding window closes.

---

**A practice or PCP appears in the Bottom layer with low recapture rates and high open gaps. What should I do?**
If that same provider also appears in the Action layer's Practice and PCP Prioritization section, you have confirmation that they are both a significant driver of underperformance and a high-priority intervention target. Escalate outreach to those providers immediately and use the specific open coding gaps identified in the Bottom layer to focus the conversation.

---

**A disease category has the lowest recapture rate and the most open gaps. How do I turn that into action?**
Navigate to the Action layer's Member-Level Targeting List and filter by that disease category to identify the specific members who need outreach. Then use the Gap Closure Operational Breakdown to confirm which visit type and provider channel are most effective for closing that category's gaps — structuring your outreach campaign around both will maximize efficiency.

---

**Members in a specific attribution status have elevated open gaps and low recapture rates. Does that affect how I reach out to them?**
Yes — attribution status shapes both where gaps accumulate and how outreach must be designed. Navigate to the Action layer's Member Segment Outreach Structure to see how members in that status are concentrated and what engagement approach is recommended. Unattributed or newly attributed members, for example, typically require a different contact strategy than continuously attributed members.

---

**What does the Period Mode filter do, and how should I use it?**
Period Mode switches all metrics between YTD (year-to-date, accumulating from January through the selected month) and Rolling (a fixed trailing window regardless of calendar year). Always confirm your Period Mode selection before comparing numbers with a colleague — a YTD view and a Rolling view for the same month will produce different totals and rates.

---

**Why do my numbers differ from my colleague's even though we are looking at the same page?**
The most common causes are mismatched filter selections — particularly Year, Month, Period Mode, or Payer. Always set Year first, then Month, then Period Mode before applying any population filters. Ask your colleague to share their exact filter state; a single difference in Attribution Status or LOB can produce materially different member counts and recapture rates.

---

**What does the Attribution Status filter do, and why does it matter for risk capture analysis?**
Attribution Status segments the population by how members are linked to a provider or care team — for example, continuously attributed, newly attributed, or unattributed. Because recapture rates and open gap rates behave very differently across these segments, always apply this filter when diagnosing why a practice or payer is underperforming, rather than assuming the issue is uniform across the full population.

---

**What does LOB (Line of Business) mean in this dashboard?**
LOB stands for Line of Business and corresponds to the specific contract or product type — such as Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, or a commercial risk arrangement — under which members are enrolled. Risk models, recapture targets, and coding windows vary by LOB, so filtering to a single LOB is essential when comparing performance across payers or setting intervention priorities for a specific contract.
